Offline-First Platforms & Learning Science

What actually works in low-connectivity education — measured, RCT-tested — and the learning-science foundations LOG's guidance layer should be built on.

M-Shule +0.46 SD literacy Funda Wande +0.5 SD (2yr) Retrieval practice g=0.50 Nudges: 6/10 null

The proven playbook for low-connectivity regions: local content first, sync when possible, phones as the delivery device, and structured teacher support as the multiplier. LOG's architecture (PWA + IndexedDB 24h cache + sync-queue + .logsync sneakernet) already mirrors the winners.

GOLD STANDARD RCT

Funda Wande — the strongest structured-pedagogy evidence

  • • Eastern Cape RCT (SA-TIED WP156): +0.17 SD reading proficiency after 1 year = 20–27% of a year's learning (Grade 2); in-service coaching at 1:5 coach:school; 90–93% of teachers used materials daily.
  • • Limpopo RCT (Feb 2023, 120 schools, 3 arms): +0.5 SD in both reading and math after 2 years in Materials+Training+Teacher-Assistant arm — the largest foundational gains ever measured in South Africa.
  • • Key insight: tech (pre-loaded flash drives, videos) was the carrier, not the intervention. Structured materials + teacher support are what moved learning.
  • → LOG implication: content packaging + teacher-facing support materials are the intervention; the app is the carrier.
M-SHULE RCT

M-Shule (Kenya) — adaptive SMS LMS: +0.46 SD at $22.70/SD

  • • Adaptive SMS LMS on any feature phone; ~20,000 households, 30 counties; US$2–5 per learner/month; works where 25% of Kenyans lack electricity and 77.5% lack internet.
  • • RCT (Angrist et al., 2024, BJET; 2,600 students, Tusome content): +0.46 SD literacy (d=0.24) at US$22.70 per SD gained — better cost-effectiveness than average personalised-learning apps (~0.18 SD).
  • • 0.181 learning-adjusted years (LAYS) per learner in EdTech Hub evaluation; 1M+ learning messages delivered.
  • → LOG implication: adaptive, level-matched practice beats one-size content; and it can be done at feature-phone grade if needed.
SCALE REFERENCES

DIKSHA (India) — the print-to-digital bridge at 600M+ scale

  • • 600M+ "energised textbooks" with 12B+ QR codes linking to content in 35+ languages; NDEAR open architecture.
  • • Works fully offline: content bundles (.ecar via SD card), QR scanning offline, Android 5.1+; per-state dashboards for QR scans, downloads, distinct devices.
  • • The reference model for print-to-digital bridging in low-connectivity settings — exactly Nepal's textbook-centric reality.
  • → LOG implication: QR codes on school materials → offline lesson content is a cheap, proven adoption wedge in Nepal.
COMMUNITY HUBS

Ideas Box / Kolibri / Kiwix — offline content at the edge

  • • Ideas Box (Libraries Without Borders): pop-up offline server + solar + Wi-Fi; 191 boxes in 25 countries; Burundi evaluation: +23% greater academic improvement than regular classrooms (Grade 4: French +31.1% vs 9.4%; Math +38.45% vs 5.5%).
  • • Ideas Cube equips 70 rural schools in Senegal with zero internet.
  • • Kolibri/Kiwix: canonical offline content-packaging platforms (bundled downloads, local-area distribution).
  • • Rumie: 2,100 six-minute "learning bytes" for low bandwidth; SMS distribution reached 42,000 users; 87% of 515 surveyed Pakistanis wanted phone-delivered educational content.
  • • The Future is Offline (LWB+Kiron): MOOCs on SD cards, offline app, certification validated via SMS.
  • → LOG implication: LOG's .logsync sneakernet aligns with the proven "file transfer + local import" pattern — keep it first-class.
CAUTION

What the evidence says NOT to do

  • • Nudges often backfire: EdTech Hub synthesis (2024) of 10 West Africa nudge-messaging studies: 6 had negative/null/insufficient effects; SMS nudges backfired on caregivers without formal schooling; over-frequent caregiver monitoring demotivated teachers; cost ~US$7.56/child. Low cost ≠ cost-effective.
  • • Hardware alone is a Bad Buy: GEEAP "Smart Buys" (13,000+ studies): Great Buys = information campaigns, structured pedagogy, teaching at the right level (TaRL); Bad Buys = hardware alone, inputs alone.
  • • Engagement collapses without structure: Shupavu291 (Kenya SMS): under 25% of students returned after joining; usage spikes during holidays/exams; no formal learning-gain evidence at scale.
  • • Implementation capacity, not technology, is the binding constraint: Khan Academy UP RCT (NBER w34683): a lab-in-charge raised usage 7.2 → 47.4 min/week (6.6x) and math +0.47 SD in 31 weeks. "The central constraint is not technology or content, but organizational structures."

MOST COST-EFFECTIVE

Phone tutoring: 0.89 SD per US$100 (Botswana RCT, Nature Human Behaviour 2022)

  • • 4,500 households: SMS alone ≈ no effect; SMS + weekly 15–20-min teacher phone call = +0.12 SD, or 0.89 SD per US$100 — among the most cost-effective learning interventions measured anywhere.
  • • Philippines (IPA replication): +40% math gains at ~US$12/child = 3.4 LAYS per $100; government-led delivery as effective as NGO-led.
  • • Angrist et al. (2023, multi-country RCT incl. Nepal): phone tutoring delivers "up to four years of high-quality instruction for $100."
  • → LOG implication: LOG guidance + teacher phone follow-up = a documented, Nepal-included intervention pathway.
ADAPTIVE SOFTWARE

Mindspark (India) — best-evidenced adaptive software in LMICs

  • • Delhi efficacy RCT (AER 2019, 619 students, 4.5 months): +0.37σ math, +0.23σ Hindi; parents paid INR 200/month (~$3); projected <US$2/student at scale.
  • • Rajasthan scale-up RCT (40 schools, ~6,500 students, 18 months): +0.22σ math, +0.20σ Hindi = 50–66% productivity gain at ~US$78/pupil over two years; effects rank ~90th percentile of LMIC RCTs.
  • • "Usage time is a low-cost proxy for implementation quality."
  • • Scale: 2,200+ government schools, 266,000+ students across 13 states (2024–25).
SMARTPHONE-BASED

Chimple (Haryana RCT 2022–23): games on parents' phones, teacher-assigned

  • • Game-based foundational learning on parents' smartphones, teacher-assigned at home; offline-capable; ~90% of households had a smartphone.
  • • +0.25 SD math (0.37 SD Grade 1); +0.14–0.30 SD English; 1 hr/week on app ≈ 0.24 SD.
  • • Effects depended on teachers using the formative dashboard — teacher visibility drives outcomes.
  • → LOG implication: teacher dashboard of student practice = the multiplier (matches CSF finding: teachers as learning agents +11% super-active users).
COST REALITY

Cost benchmarks — what per-learner economics say

  • • UNICEF/World Bank: US$48/student/year = universal digital learning 2021–2030; LICs spend only ~$55/student/year total; LMICs $309.
  • • GEEAP cost-benefit (2023): structured pedagogy + TaRL ≈ $18/student/year (BCR 65:1); TaRL $12.50–23.43/child; self-led tech median $15/student/yr.
  • • Affordability ceiling: non-recurrent finance is <US$10/student/year in some Commonwealth LICs; tablets cost $50–100/child/yr — "no scaling of technology will reduce that cost enough"; own-device mobile is the only path under $10.
  • • Eneza (Kenya SMS): 10 KSh/week (~$0.10) via airtime billing; 10M+ learners reached since 2013 — but the <25% return rate is the honest caveat.
  • → LOG implication: own-device mobile + airtime-cheap payloads is the only economic path for Nepal's community schools.
ENGINEERING BUDGETS

RESPECT standards — concrete budgets to design against

  • • 15 minutes of use must consume <25MB; installed size ≤25% above Google guidelines; offline SSO; background sync via WorkManager; no Play Services dependency (UstadMobile RESPECT).
  • • Google "Build for Billions": assume 2G; offline-first with local DB as source of truth; queue outbound requests; WebP at render size; never refetch; text-before-media sequencing.
  • • Android Go: entry-level phones 15% faster app launch, preloaded apps 50% smaller, Chrome Data Saver ~600MB saved/user/year.
  • • Data budgets for Nepal: 1GB = NPR 300–500 (~$2.2–3.7); many rural households earn <NPR 20,000/month. A 25MB/15-min budget ≈ NPR 12/session — must be smaller for daily school use.

CORE ENGINE

The learning mechanisms LOG should build on

  • • Retrieval practice (test-enhanced learning): g=0.50 — quizzing beats re-reading. Practice quizzes are not just exam prep; they ARE the learning.
  • • Feedback: d=0.73 (Hattie) — immediate, task-level feedback is among the most powerful interventions. LOG's instant practice feedback maps here.
  • • Spaced repetition: distributed practice outperforms massed; algorithms (SM-2 style) make it automatic — LOG streak/practice-schedule logic should be spaced, not daily-firehose.
  • • Personalisation / teach-to-the-level: Major et al. meta (16 RCTs, 53,029 learners): +0.18 SD overall; adaptive personalisation = +0.35 SD — personalisation is the mechanism that works.
  • • Interleaving (mixed practice) beats blocking — worth encoding in question-set design.
  • • Growth-mindset / supportive framing: "This area could use more practice" (LOG's core tone) aligns with feedback research — feedback on process, not person.
META-WARNING

Meta-evidence: how technology is used matters more than what technology is used

  • • Rodriguez-Segura (2020, 67 studies): cost-effective clusters = tech-enabled behaviour change, instruction improvement, self-led learning (+0.275 SD median, ~$15/yr); access-to-technology alone = low cost-effectiveness.
  • • UNESCO GEM Report 2023: only 7% of edtech companies had run RCTs; TALIS 2018: only 43% of lower-secondary teachers felt prepared to use tech for teaching; "plug-in gaps" procurement is the norm; benefits concentrated among advantaged.
  • • GEEAP verdict: "how technology is used matters more than what technology is used."
  • → LOG implication: never sell the tech; sell the workflow. Teacher dashboard + guided practice + parent visibility = the use case that works.

Design Rules distilled for LOG

RULE 01

Practice = retrieval, not re-reading

Quizzing is the learning engine (g=0.50). Every activity should end in retrieval practice with instant, task-level feedback (d=0.73) — not "watch and done."

RULE 02

Spaced, not firehose

Distributed practice beats massed. The practice scheduler should space reviews over days/weeks (SM-2 style) and reflect that honestly in the learning journey.

RULE 03

Personalise to the level

Adaptive teach-to-the-level = +0.35 SD vs +0.18 SD for generic tech. LOG's per-learner activity status must drive what practice a learner sees next — real data, no hallucination.

RULE 04

Teacher dashboard is the multiplier

Chimple/Mindspark/CSF all show outcomes hinge on teacher visibility and assignment. The moderator view must make "who needs what practice" one glance — this is the actual product.

RULE 05

Sell the workflow, not the tech

85% implementation failure rate is about conditions. LOG onboarding = structure + materials + teacher support (Funda Wande pattern), not feature demos.

RULE 06

No engagement nudges that backfire

6/10 nudge studies null/negative; caregiver monitoring can demotivate. Nudge sparingly, supportively, and only with real data — never spam.

RULE 07

Content packs, not per-resource fetches

DIKSHA .ecar / Kolibri bundles / Kiwix ZIM pattern: bundle content for offline import. LOG's .logsync import already fits; extend it to lesson content packs.

RULE 08

QR codes as the print bridge

12B QR codes on 600M textbooks is the proven adoption wedge in textbook-centric systems like Nepal's. QR on school materials → offline lesson = cheap and magical.

RULE 09

Budget: <25MB/15-min session, wifi-only media

RESPECT standards are the testable budget. Media downloads on wifi-only with explicit size UI; payloads gzip/Brotli (70–90% smaller); WebP images.

RULE 10

Honest usage is a feature, not a bug

Shupavu291's <25% return rate was discovered by honest analytics. LOG should surface real engagement (including zero) to teachers — honest zeros are the ethical and effective choice.

RULE 11

Phone-call follow-up workflow

0.89 SD/$100 with weekly teacher calls (RCT includes Nepal). LOG's teacher-facing "who to call" list — driven by real progress data — operationalizes the best-cost intervention.

RULE 12

Cost ceiling discipline

Self-led tech median $15/learner/yr; Nepal community schools can't exceed it. Own-device mobile + zero-infrastructure = the only path under $10. Design the cost model into the product.
