THE IRON GATE

SEE / NEB exam culture defines everything

  • • 512,471 students sat SEE 2026 across 1,966 centres; ~66,000 sat grade-improvement exams; 145,507 in the "non-graded" category.
  • • 2026 pass rate 65.98%; prior years "hovered below 50%" (Kathmandu Post editorials).
  • • Rote memorization is the pedagogy of record: students "master the typical 'Ten Sets Collections' and mug up every answer" (KP 2018); a grade-10 student called SEE "a form of mental torture" and described cheating "as a survival mechanism" (Rising Nepal 2025).
  • • Cheating is organized via WhatsApp: invigilators arrested in Bara/Sunsari feeding answers via WhatsApp groups; 1,236 phones confiscated in SEE 2080; papers leaked within 8–12 minutes of exam start.
  • • Tuition migration: 500+ grade-10 students in Bajhang migrated to Chainpur tuition centres by November — schools cancelled classes for lack of students.
  • → LOG implication: exam-prep content (model questions, sample papers, practice quizzes) is the highest-demand, highest-urgency content category in Nepal.

TEACHER CRISIS

Teacher shortage, workload & strike culture

  • • ~180,103 teachers serve 4.38M community-school students (24:1) — with a projected shortfall of 100,000 teachers in 3 years; 3,424 schools have fewer than 20 students; ~40% of community schools lack a full-time principal.
  • • Kalikot math post advertised 19 times in 7 months with no applicants; a Khotang school has 9 students and 2 untrained teachers.
  • • Nationwide teacher strikes (July 2025) instructed teachers to boycott administrative duties including "checking answer sheets, publishing results"; April 2025 teachers refused to mark SEE answer sheets for weeks.
  • • "Checking copies" consumes time that "may assure parents that teachers are looking at children's work, but does next to nothing to improve learning" — leaving "one or two free periods a day" for planning (Nepali Times).
  • • CAS (Continuous Assessment System) paperwork is a documented burden: "heavy workload, large class sizes, lack of formal training, insufficient time for documentation" (Mangal Research Journal 2025).
LANGUAGE

The English-medium debate & 124+ languages

  • • 70% of parents consider EMI important; 52% of parents prefer English-medium vs 47% of students and 40% of teachers (NepJOL survey, 200 participants).
  • • But EMI doesn't raise learning: "enrollment increased fourfold after English medium… it hasn't brought much change in students' learning" (Syangja teacher, KP 2023); MILE/UNICEF: Nepali-medium children achieved better grades in Nepali, English and Science.
  • • 32% of parents and 37% of teachers report EMI harms confidence/participation; 33% of parents and 35% of students cite lack of educational resources in Nepali.
  • • Supreme Court interim order (May 2025) against a school fining students for speaking Nepali on campus.
  • • SEE science questions "lose half the question in translation" between English and Nepali versions.
  • • Nepal: 124+ languages, 125 ethnic groups (NSO 2021); 15 mother-tongue categories have CDC materials.
  • → LOG implication: bilingual (Nepali/English) UI + content, with translation parity — never machine-garbage.
PARENTS

Parents judge by grades and textbooks

  • • "Most parents – if they are engaged at all – use the textbook to gauge whether their child is making adequate progress" (Nepali Times 2026); parents "await their children's GPAs to flaunt on social media."
  • • Communication is the weakest link in the whole system: in a 390-student study, "systemic communication" was the top problem (34.1% of variance) — teacher↔parent, school↔parent, local government↔school all below 3.25/5.
  • • The study recommends a "digital parent portal (attendance, progress, and exam results)" with mandatory monthly parent-teacher meetings.
  • • The traditional channel is the paper diary — "communication book or school diary" is still the norm nationwide.
  • → LOG implication: a parent view with attendance + progress + results is a documented, evidence-backed feature need.

THE GAP

Coverage is ahead of school connectivity

  • • NTA MIS (Chaitra 2082): 30.8M broadband subscriptions; mobile broadband = 88.7% (27.37M subs); 4G = 26.6M users (91.20% population penetration); internet user density 144%.
  • • But Flash Report 2082/25-26 (CEHRD/IEMIS): broadband internet in schools: 20.1% baseline → 60% target; electricity 52.6%→79.1%; ICT equipment 46.8%→68.2%. (Economic Survey claims 85.4% by different definitions — flagged.)
  • • World Bank ETRI (330 schools): two-thirds report connectivity, but only 20% have sufficient connected devices, <30% have sufficient bandwidth/stability, only 12% believe government help exists for connectivity failures.
  • • ETRI direct recommendation: "Consider EdTech solutions that do not require constant connectivity (offline)."
  • • Village reality: "Buddha School may not get wired internet access for another decade"; schools with internet in the village but not connected to it; 2015 earthquake destroyed 90% of schools in some districts.

DEVICES

Device reality: shared, low-end, family-owned

  • • ~70% of Nepali devices are low-end Androids; ~45% of users are women, disproportionately lost due to shared devices.
  • • India's CSF data (close proxy): household smartphone ownership rose 36%→74.8% (2018–2022), but only ~6% of children have dedicated access; ~62% of distributed devices stayed active; teachers as learning agents raised "super-active" users +11% vs parental agents.
  • • M-Shule context: 25% of Kenyans lack electricity; 77.5% lack internet — SMS LMS still reached ~20,000 households. Nepal's 91% 4G coverage is better, but affordability and shared-device reality remain.
  • • 2021 EMIS: 50% of 28,833 public schools had electricity; 42% had computers; 19% had internet; of ~12,000 with computers, only 37% used them for teaching.
  • • ICT labs gather dust: "The vast majority of ICT labs are merely spaces for storing computers… unable to function due to a lack of skilled teachers" — 10 computers locked in a Bhojpur school for 11 years (Katmandu Journal 2026).

TRAINING GAP

Digital literacy: the bottleneck is teachers

  • • "Less than half of respondents reported receiving training on how to use ICT for teaching… training programs are not mandatory" (World Bank ETRI 2023).
  • • Teachers' poorest competencies: LMS use (mean 1.39/5), screen recording (1.39), virtual boards (1.34), digital libraries (1.34).
  • • Students rate digital tools "intriguing" at 4.09/5 but teacher use at 3.23 — a 0.86 gap (F1000Research 2026; 390 students, 73 schools).
  • • Teachers use ICT mostly for information search (45%) and browsing for schoolwork (52%), rarely for teaching or assessment (ETRI).
  • • Only 26% of principals say technical support is sufficient; 22% say devices are sufficient for instruction.
  • → LOG implication: onboarding must assume near-zero LMS experience; in-app help + teacher guides in Nepali; supportive language everywhere.
GOVERNMENT PORTAL

The government's own portal is failing — an open lane

  • • Sikai Chautari (CEHRD Moodle) used by 13,472 schools vs 24,000 target (Flash Report 2082).
  • • TechPana audit (2026-04-30): no sign-up option in the app; only 3 subjects of PDF textbooks; online-only ("completely useless for remote students without internet"); teacher materials visible inside student accounts; anyone can message students.
  • • Play Store shows 5–10k downloads vs CEHRD's claim of 64k+ (conflicting figures flagged).
  • • CEHRD now mandates "at least 5 teachers per school" register — a mandate without training support.
  • → LOG implication: reliability, real sign-up, role separation and offline capability are direct differentiators against the national platform.
MOTIVATION

Tuition culture & rote learning sap motivation

  • • ~50% of secondary students and 38% of public school students receive private tuition; parents pay even when they cannot afford it — "they have no other choices."
  • • "My father told, 'I don't know anything. You must get first division in SEE. If you need tuition for that you can take the tuition.'"
  • • School hostels made mandatory for SEE students in some community schools — enrollment-driven, not pedagogy-driven.
  • • Teachers "feel they are puppets at the hands of the parents and school administrators" (Himalayan Times op-ed by a Nepali teacher).
COMMUNITY

What teachers actually ask for

  • • Exam-prep materials and model questions (SEE first) — the highest-urgency content ask.
  • • Lesson-plan and worksheet libraries aligned to the national curriculum.
  • • Report-card and attendance tooling — they are burdened by CAS paperwork and "checking copies."
  • • Bilingual (Nepali/English) resources with translation parity.
  • • Community-driven initiatives that work: WhatsApp teacher groups (also the cheating channel — the tool isn't evil, the structure needs honesty), YouTube channels (NCED Virtual), payroll fix guides shared on Facebook "Teachers Parliament" — teachers self-organize around practical help.
  • • Notable: the NEB re-evaluation scandal — until 2025 evaluators checked only 20% of answer sheets, "marking many students as failed, despite performance worthy of an 'A Plus'" (Republica 2025). Trust in official grading is low; transparent progress data is trust-building.

Nepal's edtech field is small, Kathmandu-centric, and mostly online-only — LOG's offline-first posture is a differentiator, not a me-too feature.

Design Rules distilled for LOG

RULE 01

Offline is the product, not the fallback

~20% of schools have baseline internet; ETRI explicitly recommends offline solutions. LOG's fetchWithCache/sync-queue is already the right architecture — keep it un-bypassable.

RULE 02

Exam-prep content first

SEE is the iron gate and tuition is near-universal. Model questions, sample papers, practice quizzes with instant feedback are the highest-urgency content category in Nepal.

RULE 03

Reduce the paperwork burden

CAS documentation and "checking copies" consume teachers' planning time. Attendance + assessment tooling that auto-generates report cards is a workload win teachers will adopt.

RULE 04

Bilingual by default, parity enforced

Nepali and English UI + content; SEE translation failures show the cost of sloppy parity. Never machine-translate guidance into garbled Nepali.

RULE 05

Parent portal with attendance + progress + results

Documented as the weakest link (34.1% of variance in a 390-student study) with a direct recommendation for a digital parent portal. The paper diary is the incumbent — beat it.

RULE 06

Assume zero LMS experience

Teachers rate LMS competency 1.39/5. Onboarding must be guided, in Nepali, with supportive language — a 5-minute first-run flow, not a manual.

RULE 07

Low-end, shared-device support

~70% low-end Androids, ~6% dedicated access. Small payloads, wifi-only media downloads, session handling that survives device sharing (clear local data on logout — already in F5 fix).

RULE 08

Integrate OLE Nepal content, don't compete

E-Pustakalaya (9,361 books) and E-Paath (540+ modules) are CC-licensed and offline-capable with 1,700+ servers deployed. LOG becomes the guidance layer over the best content in Nepal.

RULE 09

Trust-building transparency

NEB re-evaluation scandal (20% of sheets checked) + Sikai Chautari role leaks = trust deficit. LOG's honest zeros, real derived analytics and strict RBAC are trust features.

RULE 10

Anti-cheating by design, not by surveillance

WhatsApp-organized cheating is systemic; proctoring tech backfires (biometrics, bias). LOG: practice modes with honest progress, varied questions, no cameras, no biometrics.

RULE 11

Teacher-first community features

Nepali teachers self-organize in WhatsApp/Facebook around practical help. In-app lesson-sharing, worksheet libraries and moderated teacher groups channel that energy productively.

RULE 12

Scale-aware design for tiny schools

293 community schools have fewer than 5 students; 3,424 have fewer than 20. Everything must work for a 5-student school with one phone — no per-seat minimums, no admin burden.
