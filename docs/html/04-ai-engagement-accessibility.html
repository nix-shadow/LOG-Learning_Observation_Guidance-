THE ANCHOR RCT

Khanmigo AI tutor: +0.15 SD in a WestEd randomized trial

  • • WestEd RCT (2025): Khanmigo students gained +0.15 SD math achievement over control in a school-year trial — modest but real, in the same range as personalized-learning benchmarks (+0.18 SD).
  • • Key implementation findings: effects concentrate among students who actually use the AI regularly; teacher training on the tool matters; usage is the precondition.
  • • Khan Academy UP RCT (NBER w34683): the constraint is organizational, not the AI — a lab-in-charge raised usage 7.2 → 47.4 min/week and math +0.47 SD.
  • → LOG implication: AI value = guidance + feedback derived from real learner data. No LLM content generation (AGENTS.md §1) — but rule-based, data-derived "next practice" suggestions are the same mechanism, offline-capable, and honest.
MARKET SHIFT

2025–26: AI is table-stakes, but trust is the differentiator

  • • AI-assisted authoring, AI coaching, and AI insights are now named in most 2026 LMS buying decisions (Docebo, TalentLMS TalentCraft, Absorb).
  • • Teacher AI assistants: 4.4M+ signups across major tools; AI lesson-planning is the top teacher use case (Tech&Learning / teacher-survey data).
  • • But the Reddit/G2 voice is skeptical: "What was the AI trained on…?" — data-retention clauses, training-data capture and hallucinated feedback are the top trust killers.
  • • UNESCO GEM 2023: only 7% of edtech companies had run RCTs on their tools — "AI-powered" claims mostly lack evidence.
  • • The honest-AI position: LOG's guidance is deterministic, derived from actual backend data (AGENTS.md §1) — that is a trust feature in a market full of hallucination risk.

The science is clear: game mechanics work only when they change learning behaviour, not when they're decoration. Duolingo's own data is the best-public case study — it A/B tests everything.

WHAT WORKS

Mechanism-level gamification evidence

  • • Streaks are the most replicated retention mechanic in consumer learning (Duolingo's streak data, Habitica community studies): they convert "habit" into identity — but only when forgiving (streak freezes reduce abandonment).
  • • Immediate feedback + progress visibility (d=0.73 feedback effect): the "gamification" that survives RCT scrutiny is mostly fast, visible progress — which is really learning science wearing a game costume.
  • • Practice-spaced "chest" style rewards (SM-2 + reward) beat massed sessions in app-retention A/Bs; reward after completing scheduled review, not for time-on-app.
  • • Social comparison works for high performers, backfires for low: leaderboards lift the top third, demotivate the bottom — use "personal best" framing instead (growth-mindset language matches LOG's supportive tone).
  • • Chimple Haryana RCT: game-based app with teacher assignment produced +0.25 SD math — the games worked because they were assigned and monitored, not because of badges.
WHAT FAILS

Badge-wall gamification — the decoration trap

  • • Points/badges/leaderboards alone show null or negative effects in most rigorous studies (LMS gamification meta-analyses): rewards for completion ≠ rewards for learning.
  • • Extrinsic rewards can crowd out intrinsic motivation when tied to grades rather than effort (self-determination theory evidence).
  • • Animation/confetti spam is remembered as "childish" by teens — the anti-pattern the LOG codebase already rejected (demo confetti deleted in F11 fix).
  • • Shupavu291 caution: gamified SMS quizzes still lost >75% of joiners without teacher structure — gamification is not retention insurance.
PRACTICE

What this means for a school LMS in Nepal

  • • School context adds structure: streaks + teacher assignment + parent visibility = the retention triangle (Chimple + CSF evidence).
  • • SEE prep is inherently motivating (the iron gate) — the game is "closer to ready", not "higher on a board".
  • • Data-light design: gamification state must survive offline (IndexedDB) and sync — a streak that lies offline is worse than no streak.
  • • Honest zeros: "0-day streak" shown honestly beats fabricated momentum (AGENTS.md §1.3).
LEGAL + ETHICAL FLOOR

WCAG 2.2 AA — with the contrast backlog in mind

  • • WCAG 2.2 (Oct 2023) is the current bar; Level AA is the minimum for education platforms — including 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 UI components, focus visibility, and 24px/44px target sizes.
  • • LOG backlog item C6 flags the brand-teal/amber contrast problem (≈2:1) — the research confirms it's a real WCAG 1.4.3 failure, not a preference.
  • • r/Professors evidence: ADA-accommodated students "find it difficult to navigate" Canvas; proctoring is "ableist" for timed exams — accessibility is also a market differentiator.
  • • Screen-reader support: semantic landmarks, aria-labels (LOG already added many in F13), live-region announcements for sync/queued states.
  • • Nepal context: low-vision users, aging teachers, shared low-end devices — large-text mode and high-contrast mode are cheap and high-value.
LANGUAGE

Nepali typography & multilingual UI — done right or not at all

  • • Fonts: Nepali (Devanagari) needs fonts with full conjunct rendering and correct line-height; system Devanagari fonts vary badly across low-end Android. Preload a good open font (e.g., Noto Sans Devanagari subset) — small, offline-safe.
  • • Language switching: full Nepali/English UI parity; never a half-translated screen. Machine-translated guidance = the SEE-translation complaint ("lose half the question in translation") repeated at UI scale.
  • • 124+ languages: mother-tongue support matters (CDC has 15 mother-tongue categories); start Nepali + English with the architecture ready for more.
  • • Bidi/numbers: Nepali numerals (०१२…) vs Devanagari-context number handling; dates in BS (Bikram Sambat) calendar as an option — school systems run on BS.
  • • Voice/audio: for low-literacy parents, audio announcements in Nepali (recorded, not TTS-garbled) are a documented need (parent-communication studies).
INCLUSION

Nepal-specific accessibility needs

  • • Nepali Sign Language resources exist for grades 1–6 (OLE Nepal Sanketik E-Paath); deaf schools in Surkhet rolling out NSL activities (EAEN project) — video-first content works.
  • • Braille: CEHRD prints braille textbooks for grants, but there is no digital standard — LOG should not promise more than it can deliver (honesty principle).
  • • Low-bandwidth accessibility: text-only mode and reduced-motion mode are both accessibility AND data-savings features — one implementation, two wins.
  • • OLE Nepal i-OCR exists for converting Nepali docs to accessible text — integration candidate.

Design Rules distilled for LOG

RULE 01

Guidance from data, not from an LLM

The +0.15 SD AI-tutor effect comes from real feedback loops. LOG's rule-based guidance on real backend data achieves the same mechanism offline, with zero hallucination and zero training-data capture.

RULE 02

Streaks, spaced reviews, personal bests

Adopt the mechanisms with evidence: forgiving streaks, spaced-review rewards, "personal best" framing. Skip leaderboards (demotivate low performers) and badge walls.

RULE 03

Gamification state must sync honestly

Offline streaks must queue and reconcile like everything else (sync-queue). A streak shown optimistically that disappears on sync is a trust violation.

RULE 04

Fix the contrast backlog (C6)

Brand teal/amber at ~2:1 fails WCAG 1.4.3. Token-level palette work is a legal floor in education contexts, and low-vision users include Nepal's aging teacher workforce.

RULE 05

Nepali-first UI, never half-translated

Full Nepali/English parity; preloaded Devanagari font subset; BS calendar option; audio in recorded Nepali for low-literacy parents.

RULE 06

Reduced-motion + text-mode = data savings

One implementation serves accessibility and the 25MB/session budget. Respect prefers-reduced-motion (framer-motion already supports it).

RULE 07

Focus visibility & 44px targets

WCAG 2.2 2.4.11/2.5.8: keyboard-visible focus and target-size minimums — also correct for shared-tablet classroom use with young learners.

RULE 08

Audio-first parent communication

Recorded Nepali audio announcements solve both literacy and connectivity constraints for parents (documented weak link, 34.1% variance).

RULE 09

NSL/video-first for deaf students

OLE Nepal's NSL E-Paath proves demand; LOG should integrate NSL-tagged content rather than rebuild it.

RULE 10

No timed-exam ableism

Practice quizzes shouldn't punish speed; offer untimed modes and extra-time settings by learner need (documented proctoring failure mode).

RULE 11

Audit-friendly AI claims

If LOG ever ships "AI features", they must be named exactly for what they are (rule-based guidance), with no training-data capture and no hallucination risk — the trust differentiator vs. the 731%-growth hype wave.

RULE 12

Celebrate effort, not just results

Supportive-language guidance ("This area could use more practice") + effort-based rewards matches feedback science (d=0.73) and avoids grade-linked extrinsic-motivation crowding.
